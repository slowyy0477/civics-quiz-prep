## 1. Core definitions (learn word-for-word)
| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Civics** | The study of the **rights, duties and responsibilities of citizens** in relation to the **state and society** — how governments operate, how laws are made and enforced, how individuals and communities contribute to governance. Promotes **democracy, justice, equality, active citizenship**. |
| **Citizenship** | Being a **legally recognised member of a country** — a **legal status + social contract**. Rights: voting, protection by the state. Duties: obeying laws, paying taxes. Types: **birthright** (place of birth) and **naturalised** (after conditions — residency, language proficiency). |
| **Active citizenship** | Involvement of individuals in **public life and affairs** at local, national and international levels; **goes beyond legal status** — proactive contribution, not just obeying the law. |
| **Civic engagement** | Ways citizens participate in the **political, social and economic life** of their communities; **individual + collective action** on issues of public concern; **formal** (voting) or **informal** (volunteering); essential for the **health of a democracy**; **broader** than active citizenship. |
| **Community engagement** | Working **collaboratively with community groups** on issues affecting **their own** well-being, building relationships and partnerships for **sustainable change**; **a specific form of civic engagement**. |
| **Volunteering** | Offering services **for free** to benefit a community or cause (disaster relief, educational services, health initiatives). Example: blood donation & rehabilitation drives. |
| **Advocacy** | Efforts to **influence public policy and resource allocation** (lobbying, public campaigns, legal actions); tools: petitions, public speaking, media. |
| **Social activism** | Practice of **vigorous actions/involvement** for **political or social goals** (environmental protection, human rights, gender equality) via peaceful protests, sit-ins, online campaigns. |
| **Media literacy** | Ability to **access, analyse, evaluate and create** media; recognises bias, evaluates sources; separates credible info from **fake news**. |
| **Fake news** | **False or misleading information presented as news** → spreads misinformation, influences elections, incites violence. |
| **Citizen journalism** | Collection, dissemination and analysis of news **by the general public**, especially online (smartphones, social media, blogs). |
| **Digital campaigns** | Online tools + social media to advocate causes and mobilise support — hashtags, viral videos, online petitions (#MeToo, climate activism). |

## 2. The 5+5 lists (Chapter 1)
- **Importance of civic/community engagement:** strengthens democracy · builds stronger communities · empowers citizens · addresses social issues · encourages civic responsibility (common good).
- **Key concepts in civics:** **Citizenship · Democracy · Rule of Law · Civic Virtue · Public Participation.**
  - **Democracy** = power vested in the **people**, ruling directly or through **freely elected representatives**.
  - **Rule of Law** = **all** members of society, **including those in government**, are equally subject to the law.
  - **Civic Virtue** = habits for community success — **honesty, responsibility, respect for others**.
  - **Public Participation** = citizens involved in **decision-making** of government and public institutions.

## 3. civil vs civics
- **civil = adjective** (non-military, non-religious, non-criminal): civil rights (= political & social freedom and equality), **civil law** (= **private disputes** between individuals/organisations), civil society, civil engineering.
- **civics = noun**: the **study** of rights and duties of citizenship + how government and society function (constitution, voting, responsibilities).

## 4. Forms of civic engagement
**Voting** (most fundamental) · **community service** · **advocacy** · **+ petitioning, public meetings, protests.**

## 5. Pakistan case studies (know the years)
- **Lawyers' Movement 2007-2009** → restoration of **judiciary independence**; media mobilised public support; outcome → democratic momentum + international attention.
- **2005 earthquake** → citizens' participation in **disaster relief**.
- **Youth-led initiatives for educational reform**.
- **Constitution of Pakistan 1973** → every adult citizen **18+** has the right to vote; **General Elections 2024** → millions voted for the **National Assembly** and **Provincial Assemblies**. Agencies named: **NADRA, ECP**.

## 6. Media
- **Functions:** **watchdog** (exposes corruption, ensures accountability) · **agenda-setter** · **platform for public debate**.
- **Traditional** (newspapers, TV): informs, shapes opinion (editorials, investigative journalism). *Challenge:* **ownership + bias in framing**.
- **Social** (Twitter, Facebook): real-time engagement, mobilisation, information sharing. *Challenges:* **misinformation + echo chambers**.
- **Bias types:** **omission · selection of sources · story framing.** Fix: diversify sources + fact-check.
- **Verification:** fact-checking sites, cross-reference sources, check source credibility, **verify before sharing**.
- **Big 3 challenges:** **censorship** · **commercialisation** (→ sensationalism) · **misinformation**. **Solutions:** media **independence** + media **literacy**.
- **Independent media in Pakistan:** checks government power, promotes transparency; journalists face **threats, intimidation, violence**; needs protections + press freedom.
- **Future:** podcasts, YouTube, livestreaming; challenges = **digital divide, misinformation, privacy**.

## 7. Why a CS student studies civics (4)
1. **Develop responsible citizenship** — privacy rights → apps that respect users' data.
2. **Promote ethical and inclusive technology** — Urdu-based apps for farmers, not only English.
3. **Strengthen problem-solving through social awareness** — align e-governance features with NADRA/ECP/local government.
4. **Enhance leadership and teamwork** — the same skills software development needs.
